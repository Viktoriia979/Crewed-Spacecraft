Crewed Spacecraft:

Crewed spacecraft are vehicles designed to carry astronauts safely into space and back to Earth. They are used for missions such as travelling to the International Space Station, exploring the Moon, and, in the future, travelling to Mars. Crewed spacecraft are special because they are built to keep people alive in the harsh environment of space.

Crewed spacecraft use powerful rockets to launch into space and are equipped with life-support systems that provide oxygen, water, food, and a comfortable temperature for the astronauts. They also have computers, communication systems, and heat shields to protect the crew during launch, spaceflight, and re-entry into Earth's atmosphere. Some spacecraft can also dock with space stations or other spacecraft.

Examples of crewed spacecraft include Apollo, which carried astronauts to the Moon, Space Shuttle, which was used for many space missions, and Crew Dragon, which transports astronauts to and from the International Space Station. Crewed spacecraft are important because they allow humans to carry out scientific experiments, repair satellites, and explore space in ways that robots cannot.
